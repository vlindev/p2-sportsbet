# uieval — Professional UX/UI Design Critique

Only trigger if `uieval` is the entire message. Usable on mockup HTML files OR rendered/implemented pages.

## Role

Evaluate this UI as a **senior UX/UI designer** would. Give a professional design critique. Be direct and precise, do not soften criticism.

## Evaluation Dimensions

### Information architecture
- Is the right information present?
- Is anything critical missing, misplaced, or buried?
- Is the grouping and structure logical?

### Visual hierarchy
- Does the layout guide the eye in the correct order?
- Does the most important information receive the strongest visual emphasis?
- Are any elements visually over- or under-weighted?

### Clarity & cognitive load
First-glance test: could a new user understand what this screen does within **5 seconds**?
- What would they immediately understand?
- What would likely confuse them?
- Where does the interface create unnecessary cognitive load?

### Consistency & convention
- Does it follow established UI patterns users already know?
- Where does it break convention?
- If it breaks convention, is the break justified?

### Actionability
- Are the next steps obvious?
- Do CTAs (buttons, links, actions) stand out appropriately?
- Is the user clearly guided toward the primary action?

## Overall Assessment

### What works
Identify **specific elements that are well executed**, and explain why.

### What doesn't work
Identify **specific problems**, and explain:
- Which UX/UI principle is violated
- Why that matters for usability

### Priority fixes
If you could change **only 3 things**, what would they be?

Rank them and briefly explain the expected impact of each change.

## Rules

- Base your evaluation on established UX/UI principles (e.g., **Gestalt principles, Fitts's Law, Hick's Law, Nielsen's heuristics, progressive disclosure**).
- When pointing out a problem, **explicitly connect it to the principle being violated when relevant**.
- Clearly distinguish between **direct observations from the mockup** and any **inferences about the workflow or user behavior**.
- Be specific and actionable; avoid generic advice.
- **Do not change anything.** Present the evaluation and wait for user direction.
